Entry point
Not a search result. Not an AI citation. An institutional relationship.
The Toronto Humane Society adoption confirmation is a moment of high trust and high attention. Zach just committed to a living animal. He's reading every word. The single line about insurance isn't a scare tactic — it's the kind of thing a good adoption counselor would say in person.
B2B2C, not media buy
The shelter is a distribution partner, not a referral source.
This is a different infrastructure argument than Emily's flow. Not "be present in AI conversations" — "build the pipe from institutional data sources." The shelter already holds pet name, approximate DOB, sex, intake health status, vaccine history, and adopter city. The partnership just needs an API handshake and a permission model.

What travels — and what doesn't
The shelter data is real and already captured. In 2026, this handoff is technically feasible: structured adoption record + adopter email + opt-in = everything zach1 needs. What can't travel: any name Zach might have chosen (Yoshi was the shelter name, and he kept it — realistic). Post-adoption context, vet appointment, budget — those come from Zach directly.

The honest frame
The pre-education moment is the product decision, not the partnership.
The single most important line in the email: enroll before the first vet visit, or anything flagged there becomes pre-existing. Zach didn't know this. Nobody told him. The brand that tells him at the right moment — between adoption and first appointment — earns the enrollment.
